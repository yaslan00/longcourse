## Email 2, day 3

Subject: How to read us (and everyone else)

Every Long Course article has a small box with three parts: what the evidence shows, our interpretation, and a practical example. That split is the whole point. Studies show things in specific people over specific times. Interpretation is a judgement. Examples are just examples.

Each article also carries an accuracy review date at the bottom. On or before that date it gets re-read against newer evidence and either confirmed, updated with a dated note, or retired.

If you want the habit that makes the biggest difference, it is this: when a headline says a food "causes" or "prevents" something, find out whether the study was randomized and whether the risk number is relative or absolute. This piece walks through it: {{site}}/articles/how-to-read-a-nutrition-headline/
