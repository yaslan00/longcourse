# Long Course: editorial voice guide

Long Course is an independent nutrition publication written by Yiğit Aslan. The name comes from the 50 meter pool: the long course is where pacing matters more than the sprint. We write about nutrition the same way. Long horizon, honest about uncertainty, useful today.

## Who we write for

Everyday readers who train, move, cook, or simply want a calmer relationship with food. Assume a curious adult with no nutrition degree. Never talk down. Never talk in jargon without translating it in the same sentence.

## What we sound like

Conversational, clear, specific, and thoughtful. Like a well read friend who trained hard for years, went to school for this, and would rather tell you what the evidence actually says than sell you a rule.

Do:
- Open with the reader's real question or situation, in one or two plain sentences. No throat clearing.
- Use concrete numbers with units and context ("about 1.2 to 1.6 g of protein per kg per day, so roughly 100 to 130 g for an 80 kg person").
- Distinguish, explicitly and in the text, between what is well supported in humans, what is early or mechanistic, and what is our interpretation or a practical suggestion.
- Say when something is uncertain, contested, or population specific. Name the population studied.
- Use short paragraphs. Vary sentence length. Use headings that say something ("Why timing matters less than you think"), not labels ("Timing").
- Write practical examples as examples, clearly framed ("One way this could look:"), not prescriptions.
- Include one pull quote per article that is a real sentence from the piece, not a slogan.
- Cite sources inline by number and list them under "Sources and notes" with full titles, journals, years, and links. Every citation must exist and must support the sentence it is attached to.

Don't:
- No em dashes or en dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses. This is a hard rule for the whole project, including code comments and metadata.
- No formulaic intros ("In today's fast paced world"), no listicles by default, no keyword stuffing, no "unlock your potential" copy.
- No fear, no moralizing about food, no "clean" or "cheat" language, no calorie counting advice inside content about relationship with food or eating disorders.
- No invented anecdotes, quotes, patient stories, or claims about Yiğit's personal experience unless he has stated them. His swimming background may appear as perspective ("in a sport where two-a-day sessions are normal") but not as a story every time.
- No claims of clinical authority. Yiğit is a master's candidate and dietetic intern working toward the RD credential; never describe him as an RD or clinician. Content is educational, not medical advice.
- No supplement dosing, medication guidance, eating disorder treatment advice, or individualized plans. Those topics stay general and educational and are flagged for qualified human review before publication.

## Structure that works (vary it)

1. The question, stated plainly.
2. The short answer, honestly hedged.
3. What the evidence shows (human studies first; label animal, in vitro, and mechanistic work).
4. Where it gets complicated (populations, dose, timing, quality of studies).
5. What this could look like in practice (examples, not prescriptions).
6. The takeaway in two or three sentences.
7. Sources and notes.

Not every article needs every part. A short explainer can be 700 words. A substantial piece runs 1,400 to 2,200 words.

## Content lanes

- Fuel: training, recovery, hydration, energy availability for active people.
- Everyday: plain explanations of foods, nutrients, and habits.
- Headlines: how to read nutrition research and news without getting played.
- Flexibility: relationship with food, hunger, fullness, and eating without rules.

Fuel and Everyday content may discuss energy and performance. Flexibility content never sits next to weight loss messaging or calorie targets. Related reading modules must respect this separation.

## Review gates

- Standard: factual check, source check, accessibility check, no em dash check. Publishable by the automated editor under the approved policy.
- Human review required: anything touching eating disorder treatment, medication, supplement doses, individualized medical advice, or claims about specific clinical populations. These stay in draft until Yiğit or a qualified reviewer signs off.
