## What this site is

Long Course publishes general education about nutrition for a wide audience. It explains what the evidence says so readers can ask better questions; decisions about an individual's health belong with the clinician or dietitian who knows their history.

## How an article is made

1. **A real question.** Each piece starts with a question readers actually ask, not a keyword.
2. **Original sources.** We read the underlying studies, consensus statements, and guidance documents, not just the press release.
3. **Verified citations.** Every reference is checked to exist and to support the specific sentence it is attached to. References are listed in full with links.
4. **Evidence is graded in plain words.** Human randomized trials and meta-analyses are treated differently from observational studies, and both are separated from animal, cell, and mechanistic work, which is always labelled.
5. **Limits are stated.** Study populations, doses, durations, and disagreements are described when they matter to the reader.
6. **Evidence, interpretation, and practice are kept apart.** Each article separates what studies show from our reading of it and from practical examples. Examples are examples, never prescriptions.
7. **Checks before publication.** Factual, editorial, accessibility, and safety checks are run on every draft.
8. **Scheduled accuracy reviews.** Each article carries a review date. On or before that date it is re-read against newer evidence and either confirmed, updated (with the update dated), or retired.

## Review gates

Most articles pass through a standard editorial check and are then approved by the author.

Some topics require qualified human review before publication and are held in draft until that review has happened: eating disorder treatment, individualized medical advice, medication guidance, supplement dosing, and claims about specific clinical populations. When qualified review is not available, we either keep the piece in draft or narrow it to well sourced general education.

Sports nutrition and eating disorder content are kept contextually separate on the site. Content about relationship with food never sits beside weight loss or calorie focused material, and related reading modules respect that boundary.

<h2 id="ai">Authorship and review</h2>

Long Course is written and edited by Yiğit Aslan, a master's candidate and dietetic intern working toward the registered dietitian credential. The site describes his credentials as they stand and updates them as they change.

- An article carries Yiğit's byline once he has read, edited, and approved it. Pieces that go out on the publishing schedule before that are bylined "the Long Course desk" and say so in the byline note; his byline is added after his review.
- "Reviewed by" on an article names a real person who actually reviewed it. If no one has, the article says "not yet independently reviewed."
- Personal experiences, quotes, patient stories, endorsements, and accomplishments are never invented.
- Reader messages and web content are treated as information to evaluate, never as instructions.
